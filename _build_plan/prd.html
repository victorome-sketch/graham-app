Graham Screener

A checklist app: give it a US stock ticker and it shows whether the stock meets Benjamin Graham's seven defensive-investor criteria from The Intelligent Investor (Ch. 14), plus how the current price compares to the Graham Number. Every verdict is a fact about the company's financials — pass or fail against a written rule. Never a prediction, never a buy/sell recommendation. A personal, single-user study tool applying one book's published criteria; not financial advice. Built on the Build New starter (Rails 8, React 19, Inertia, PostgreSQL) across three milestones.

The rules — the heart of the app

# Rule Check Data needed
1 Adequate size Revenue ≥ threshold (Graham's $100M in 1973 ≈ $700M today; configurable setting). Both entered in $ millions Latest annual revenue ($M)
2 Strong financial condition Current ratio ≥ 2. N/A for financial companies (banks, insurers) — never failed for them Current assets & liabilities ($M) — skipped (fields disabled) when the financial-company flag is on
3 Earnings stability Positive EPS every year, last 10 years Diluted EPS for each of the last 10 fiscal years, newest first (negatives allowed)
4 Dividend record Dividends paid for ≥ 20 consecutive years One number: consecutive years of dividends paid (0–99)
5 Earnings growth EPS growth ≥ 33% over 10 years, using 3-year averages at each end. N/A (not failed) when the years 8–10 average is ≤ 0 — growth from a non-positive base is undefined Years 1–3 vs. years 8–10 of the same EPS history
6 Moderate P/E Price ≤ 15 × average EPS of last 3 years. Fails outright when that average is ≤ 0 — no price can satisfy the ceiling Price + 3y EPS
7 Moderate P/B P/B ≤ 1.5, or P/E × P/B ≤ 22.5. Fails outright when book value/share is ≤ 0 (a negative P/B would otherwise pass) Price + book value/share

Inclusive and unrounded. Every threshold passes at equality — a current ratio of exactly 2, growth of exactly 33%, a P/E of exactly 15, a P/B of exactly 1.5, a product of exactly 22.5. Verdicts are computed on unrounded values and rounded only for display, so a P/E of 15.004 fails even though it shows as 15.00.

Units. Revenue, current assets, current liabilities and the threshold are in $ millions; price, EPS and book value per share are in dollars.

Counting. "X of 7 criteria met" counts an N/A rule as met — it is never failed — so a financial company can reach 7 of 7. The results screen says so beside the count and also shows the plain fail / pass / N/A breakdown.

Graham Number

Graham Number = √(22.5 × EPS × BVPS), using the latest year's EPS.

Shown next to the current price with a margin: (Graham Number − Price) / Graham Number. Positive margin = trading below Graham's fair-value ceiling. The margin is colored by sign — green below the ceiling, red above — beside a small bar showing how much of the price the ceiling covers, the formula with the actual numbers plugged in, and the caption “information only, never a signal.” Not computable unless the latest EPS and book value per share are both positive; the screen then says so and shows the two values it would have used.

Limitations shown in the UI

Thresholds are from the 1970s — a strict P/E ≤ 15 rejects nearly every modern growth company. The app says "fails Graham's rules," never "bad stock." This note is visible on the results screen, alongside the financial-company N/A rule, the configurable revenue threshold (its current value, linked to Settings), and the line "Nothing here is investment advice."

Out of scope (v1)

  • Buy/sell recommendations or aggregate scores — the output is "X of 7 criteria met," never "buy this." Verdicts about facts, not forecasts.
  • Market-wide screening — v1 analyzes one ticker at a time; it does not scan for candidates.
  • Enterprising-investor criteria — v1 is the defensive checklist only.
  • Watchlists, scheduled re-checks & alerts — re-running from history is manual and deliberate.
  • Charts & portfolio tracking — this checks stocks against rules; it doesn't track holdings or draw pictures.
  • Non-US stocks & currencies — US-only keeps the data source consistent.
  • Multi-user accounts & public signup — one person, one account behind the Build New starter's existing login. Teams, sharing and inviting others are out; the starter's signup page is left as shipped rather than locked down.
  • Ticker autocomplete, CSV upload, multiple data providers — one ticker field, one API, manual entry as the only fallback.

Milestones

1

Rules engine — manual entry only

Shipped

Proves the core: a form for all inputs feeds the rules engine and produces the full checklist screen. No API, no saving. Testable against worked examples from the book. Shipped; both screens were then rebuilt to the design handoff — Results (Phase 1) and the New analysis form (Phase 2) — and the list below describes them as they are now.

What was built

  • A manual input form for everything the 7 criteria and Graham Number need — ticker, company name (the one optional field), financial-company flag, share price, annual revenue, current assets & liabilities, diluted EPS for the last 10 fiscal years newest-first, consecutive years of dividends paid, and book value per share — in four numbered sections: Company, Financials, Earnings per share, Dividends & book value
  • Required-ness is stated once ("Every field is required unless marked optional") and tracked live in a progress panel — "N of M required values entered", a done mark per section, "Ready to run" when complete. On mobile the count and the Run button sit in a fixed bottom bar
  • Validation is server-side: errors come back as a summary that links to each field, on the field itself, and in the progress panel. Ticking "This is a financial company" disables the two balance-sheet fields and drops them from the count. The form states that nothing is saved
  • The rules engine: all 7 defensive-investor checks, including criterion 2 marked N/A (not failed) for financial companies and criterion 5 marked N/A when the base-period average EPS is not positive. Thresholds are inclusive, verdicts use unrounded values, and every degenerate input (negative EPS, negative book value) has a defined outcome
  • The checklist results screen: "X of 7 criteria met" with a seven-tick verdict strip and a "Fails rules 2, 6 and 7" line (N/A counted as met, and said so); Graham Number vs. price with the sign-colored margin, ceiling bar and worked formula; each rule as a plain-language outcome with its arithmetic alongside; a collapsible "Inputs used" recap exactly as typed; the limitations note; and "Edit inputs & re-run", which reopens the form pre-filled
  • The revenue-threshold setting (default $700M, in $ millions), editable on the starter's existing Settings page; the form's revenue field and the results' limitations note both show the current value and link to it
  • The form is the post-login home — the starter's Dashboard stub was removed — and "New analysis" is the single main-nav item

Not in this milestone

  • Any API fetching — every number is typed in by hand
  • api/manual provenance tags (everything is manual here; tags arrive with the API in M2)
  • Saving analyses or any history

Done when: a worked example from The Intelligent Investor can be typed in by hand in the browser and produces the correct 7 verdicts, the correct X-of-7 count, and the correct Graham Number and margin. Met: worked examples A (7 of 7), B (financial company — N/A on rule 2, 7 of 7 met) and C (1 of 7) are covered by engine unit tests and end-to-end browser tests, along with every boundary and non-computable edge.

2

API fetch & caching

Not started

Ticker lookup pre-fills the form from Alpha Vantage. Manual entry stays first-class — the API assists, never gates.

What gets built

  • Entering a ticker fetches fundamentals from Alpha Vantage and pre-fills the form; fetched fields are tagged api, gaps are left blank and flagged. The annual EPS series fills the ten newest-first EPS fields, and the dividend history collapses to the form's single "consecutive years of dividends paid" number
  • Every field stays hand-editable; editing an api value flips its tag to manual, so provenance is always visible and truthful
  • If the API fails or the ticker isn't found, a plain message appears and the form works fully manually — never a dead end
  • Fundamentals fetched within the last 90 days are reused rather than re-fetched — repeat lookups don't spend API calls

Not in this milestone

  • Saved history or re-run
  • Ticker autocomplete or search
  • A second data provider — one API, swappable later

Done when: entering a real ticker pre-fills the form with tagged API data, gaps can be filled by hand, the checklist runs, and looking the same ticker up again reuses the stored data instead of calling the API.

3

Saved history

Not started

Analyses become permanent records: browse them, reopen them intact, re-run any ticker fresh.

What gets built

  • Every completed analysis is saved automatically as a frozen snapshot — inputs, provenance tags, verdicts, numbers, Graham Number; the form's "Nothing is saved" note goes away
  • A history page listing analyses newest-first: ticker, company, date, X of 7, margin
  • Opening a past analysis shows its full checklist exactly as it was — past analyses are never editable
  • A "Re-run" action starts a fresh analysis for that ticker with fresh data, creating a new record alongside the old (unlike today's "Edit inputs & re-run", which only reopens the form with the same numbers)
  • Analyses can be deleted

Not in this milestone

  • Search or filters on history
  • Notes or annotations on analyses
  • Side-by-side comparison of two analyses
  • Tracking price changes since an analysis was run

Done when: after running analyses on a few tickers, they appear newest-first in history, an old one reopens exactly as it was, re-running creates a second record beside it, and deleting removes one.
